# A model on panel data (entities seen period after period)

A panel has a **period** column (a date, or a growing whole number such as `era`) whose values come back on many rows, and often an **entity** column (an id, a ticker, a store) that comes back at each period: customers × months, shops × weeks, stocks × days. Its rows are not independent: an entity comes back every period and the rows of one period share a context (a market, a season). A random split or a shuffled k-fold then scores too well, and the model will not hold on the next period.

1. **Recognise it** (`check_panel`, with the target). Say what it found in one sentence ("574 eras × ~5,000 ids") and that random splits would overestimate the score. If the period or entity guessed is wrong, ask, then call it again with the right columns.
2. **Validate by period.** Train (`train_model`) with the validation `check_panel` returns: `panel_walk_forward`, every fold trained on past periods and tested on the next block. Keep the period and the entity out of the features (the app does it).
3. **Embargo.** When the target looks forward (a 20-day return on weekly rows), successive targets of an entity overlap: leave as many periods out before each test block. Use the embargo `check_panel` suggests and give its reason; never set it below that without saying why.
4. **Read the periods, not one number.** Quote the mean score per period, its standard deviation, the mean / std ratio (the steadiness), the share of good periods, the worst period and the longest bad streak. For numbers ranked within each period (only the order counts), the usual metric is the Spearman correlation per period. A model a little weaker on average but steadier is often the better one: say so.
5. **Leaks proper to a panel.** Read the run's panel alerts aloud: a column from the future (more linked to the next period's target than the current one, or only filled in the last periods), a score far above what the 3 best columns give alone ("check that no column is computed from the target"), an embargo shorter than the target's horizon. Name the column.
6. **Exposure.** If the predictions follow one column very closely (a high maximum exposure), the model depends on it: suggest neutralizing on those columns in the app and comparing the per-period summary before and after.
7. **Compare and tune** (`compare_models`, `tune_model`) with the same panel validation and say which criterion ranks them: mean of the periods, mean / std, or worst period. Choose mean / std or worst period when steadiness matters more than the average.
8. **Large panels.** Beyond 200 periods the app trains on one period in four (whole periods, never rows drawn inside a period), as Numerai participants often do: mention it when it applies.
9. **Limits.** A walk-forward score holds for periods like the ones tested; a change of regime breaks it. The model reflects this data only.

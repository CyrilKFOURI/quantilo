# Indicators over time

- **Start from the question.** "Revenue per month by country" fixes the measure, the aggregation, the time grain and the split. `suggest_kpis` proposes sensible ones from the columns, best score first, each with its reasons (cite the first one when you pick it: "average basket, because Order ID names the order"); `compute_kpi` computes one.
- **Aggregation.** Sums for amounts and counts, means for levels (prices, scores), distinct counts for customers. A **ratio** is the ratio of sums (total revenue / total orders), never the mean of per-row ratios. A **margin** is (revenue − cost) ÷ revenue (`ratio.kind: margin`), a **difference** revenue − cost. A price or a rate with a quantity is a **weighted average** (weight: the quantity). "% paid", "% of orders cancelled" are a **share** of a status's values (`agg: share`, `values`, optional `key`). A delay between two dates (order → ship) is the mean or median of `column` → `until`, in days.
- **Time grain.** Choose it from the date range: days for a few weeks, weeks or months for a year, months or quarters beyond. The last period is often incomplete: say so before reading a drop.
- **Comparisons.** Period over period and year over year answer different questions; seasonal data needs year over year. Shares of a total show the mix, not the growth.
- **Splits.** Keep the few largest groups; small groups move a lot by chance. A hierarchy from `suggest_kpis` (Region > State) splits by the parent, then the child; never split by two columns that say the same thing.
- **Signals.** `compute_kpi` returns `signals`, each with its rule and a sentence: unusual periods (robust z) and level shifts of the total series, cells resting on fewer than 30 values (low n), thin periods, a measure empty on more than 20 % of the rows, rows repeated exactly in an order (sums count them twice), the rate and mix effects of a rate split by a group, and Simpson's paradox. Cite the ones that change the reading (a Simpson, duplicates, a low n behind the change you report) before the figures; never call an unusual period a trend.
- **The current period.** A tile's `to_date` compares the started period at an equal date ("Nov 1–10 vs Oct 1–10 and vs Nov 1–10 last year"); use it rather than the incomplete period's value. Its `projected` value exists only for sums and counts and is a projection at the current pace, say so.
- **Saving.** Offer `save_kpi` only when the user wants to keep the indicator; it asks them first.

Report the latest value, the change and its period, and the one movement worth attention.
